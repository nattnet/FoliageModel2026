The beauty score (the dot) is round((w_H·H + w_S·S + w_C·Clean)/10, 1). Move the weights and watch every disc, its colour, and the window‑leader recompute.

These three are slices of one pie (always 100%). Pull one slice bigger and the others shrink a touch — that's the page keeping the pie whole, not a bug.

Hue weightreds present + hue variety 45%

More = reward reds & a varied colour mix; less = a single colour scores like a varied one.

Saturation weightcolour glow / purity 45%

More = reward pure, glowing colour; less = pale / hazy colour isn't penalised.

Cleanliness weight100 − dull / brown / cloud 10%

More = penalise dull / brown / cloud harder; less = dullness matters less.

total 100% · one whole pie

Why 45 / 45 / 10 by default: hue and glow are the real decision, and I don't yet know which your eye prefers — so they share the pie equally. Cleanliness is a penalty / tiebreaker (a dull day should lose points, but on a clear day everyone is clean, so it shouldn't dominate) — hence a small slice.

#3 — what each predictor measures
R red‑potential: capacity to manufacture crimson — cool above‑freezing nights (≈0–7 °C), bright days, trapped sugars, moisture, red‑capable species. Warm nights cap it (the El Niño effect).
Y yellow‑potential: carotenoid unmasking via a gradual cool‑down + gold species; hard freeze → brown.
C conditions: favourable and safe weather — low rain/wind leaf‑loss, low cloud, low abrupt frost = the booking‑risk axis.
Baselines & ENSO sources
30‑year normal 1991–2020 (WMO — World Meteorological Organization); alternative tourism median 1981–2010. Drift studies: temperate China 1982–2011 (+0.12 days/year later end‑of‑season); subtropical China 2001–2018 (+0.41 days/year later onset); Japan alpine 2011–2020 (declining brightness).
2026 ENSO: IRI (International Research Institute for Climate and Society) ≈100% El Niño from the Jul–Aug–Sep trimester; NOAA/CPC (Climate Prediction Center) ≈97% persistence to spring 2027; consensus peak of the Niño‑3.4 index (the standard El Niño sea‑temperature region) ≈3.6 °C; ≈81% chance of very‑strong in Oct–Dec.
