Section 1 · Method

How the model works — read this first

Everything below is driven by these definitions. The symbols R Y C H S are the only abbreviations used anywhere; everything else is spelled out. The full iteration history lives in Section 6.

The three lenses

  • Timing (Section 4) — does your window catch the peak, or land on green / bare?
  • Beauty (Section 3) — the realised look on the hill, scored 0–10 as a coloured dot.
  • Why (Section 3) — the forward‑looking predictors, and the Diagnosis pill that names any gap.

The beauty score & its bands

beauty score = ROUND( ((w_H·Hue + w_S·Saturation + w_C·Cleanliness) / (w_H+w_S+w_C)) / 10 , 1 )

The weights (w_H, w_S, w_C) are your dial in Section 2; the score auto‑normalises by their total. It maps to a colour:

≥ 9 — red — exceptional, peak, optimal
≥ 7 — orange — good, solid colour
≥ 5 — yellow — fair, muted or timing mismatch
≥ 3 — grey — poor, post‑peak or declining
< 3 — black — bare / snow / winter

The dial weights (Hue / Saturation / Cleanliness)

  • Hue — more = reward reds & a varied mix; less = a single colour scores like a varied one.
  • Saturation — more = reward pure, glowing colour; less = pale / hazy colour isn't penalised.
  • Cleanliness — more = penalise dull / brown / cloud harder; less = dullness matters less.

Default 45 / 45 / 10: hue and glow share the pie equally (I won't guess your taste); cleanliness is a small penalty / tiebreaker.

The predictors (R / Y / C), 0–10

  • R red‑potential — capacity to make crimson: cool above‑freezing nights (≈0–7 °C), bright days, trapped sugars, moisture, red species. Warm nights cap it.
  • Y yellow‑potential — carotenoid gold via a gradual cool‑down + gold species; a hard freeze turns it brown.
  • C conditions — favourable and safe weather: low rain/wind leaf‑loss, low cloud, low abrupt frost = the booking‑risk axis.

The five‑series timing layer (Section 4)

Each place is decomposed into five series so nothing is a vague "≈ average":

  • (i) baseline — sourced, ENSO‑neutral peak window.
  • (ii) 2025 drift‑expected = (i) + z·n(2025).
  • (iii) 2025 observed — the real year, as a marker where known.
  • (iv) 2026 drift‑expected = (i) + z·n(2026).
  • (v) 2026 model = (iv) + El Niño overlay — the headline you book on.

Gap A = (iii) − (ii): the 2025 weather / interannual anomaly. Gap B = (v) − (iv): the pure El Niño overlay (dial‑able to 0 = your neutral fallback).

Uncertainty halos & the noise floor

Every window carries a halo = noise floor (±3–5 d) + cross‑class source spread + propagated drift error (n·σ_z) + provenance term + vintage term, summed as a conservative outer bound.

Noise‑floor rule: a window tighter than ±3–5 d, or a single observed year pinned to the exact day, is below the floor = diminishing returns (the digit is weather's jitter). Adding a whole series or a halo is above the floor = never diminishing returns. So structural refinement never plateaus; the brake only bites on point‑precision.

Drift z, elevation damping & the anchor year

Site rate = regional rate × damping (1.0 lowland; 0.5 central for mountains, with a [0.3–0.7] range feeding the halo). Regional rates: Korea +0.33 d/yr; temperate China +0.12 d/yr (Liu 2016); subtropical China +0.41 d/yr end‑of‑season (Xu 2024). Where no study‑backed rate exists, a flagged proxy (≈0.25 d/yr) is used with a wide halo.

Anchor year y_op = 2005.5 (centre of the WMO 1991–2020 normal) for the central line; n = year − y_op. The 1981–2010 tourism median is not used as an anchor (legacy vintage would double‑count the trend) — kept only as a footnote. Provenance/vintage ambiguity we can't verify is carried in the halo rather than faked as a per‑site anchor.

Multi‑source interpolation (by definition‑class)

Sources are tagged by what they measure — government first‑leaf / full‑colour / leaf‑fall triplets · satellite greenness‑fall · tourism "best viewing" week · photography "peak saturation" window. Within a class the median sets the central window; across classes the spread is the halo (onset, fall and saturation don't average cleanly). So "three sources" only tightens the halo if they span classes or are recent.

Reading the Diagnosis pill

aligned ✔ the window sits on the peak. rising · pre‑peak building. timing miss · still green not turned yet. past peak · still vivid / fair on the way down, colour remains. on peak · weather risk fragile. off‑window · bare nothing left.

Baselines & ENSO (El Niño–Southern Oscillation) 2026

30‑year normal 1991–2020 (WMO — World Meteorological Organization); alternative tourism median 1981–2010.

2026 ENSO: IRI — El Niño ≈100% from the Jul–Aug–Sep trimester; NOAA/CPC — ≈97% persistence to spring 2027 and an 81% chance of very‑strong (Niño‑3.4 ≥ +2.0 °C) in Oct–Dec; IRI multi‑model — 23 of 26 models (~88%) very‑strong for Oct–Nov–Dec. Consensus peak of the Niño‑3.4 index ≈ 3.6 °C (Yale model pool), possibly the strongest on record. Relative Niño‑3.4 median ≈ 3.1 °C — same event, different yardstick. Direction robust; magnitude re‑check 6–8 weeks out.

Section 4 · Timing

Timing — five series, two gaps, one honest halo

Each place is decomposed into the five series (Section 1). Gap A measures how far last year's observation deviated from its own trend expectation; Gap B isolates the El Niño overlay. The halo is a conservative outer bound — wide where the data is thin, tight where it isn't.

Sources & verification

Drift studies — Liu (2016): temperate China end‑of‑season +0.12 d/yr (1982–2011). Xu (2024): subtropical China end‑of‑season +0.41 d/yr (2001–2018). Korea national +0.33 d/yr (last 16 years).

Section 6 · Changelog

How the model evolved — and what each step fixed

A model that doesn't explain how it arrived at itself isn't auditable. Each node records the change and the gap or bug it closed, plus the principle behind it. Newest at the top.

v0.5 · THIS BUILD

Timing layer decomposed into five series + Gap A / Gap B

Baseline → 2025 drift‑expected → 2025 observed → 2026 drift‑expected → 2026 model, with the two gaps as named diagnostics and a per‑site uncertainty halo on every window.

Closed the Seoraksan contradiction (a 29 Oct observation drawn inside a 12–25 Oct bar): the gap is now a measured +9 d anomaly, not a hidden error. Principle: separate climatological baseline, secular trend, and interannual anomaly — never smear them into one bar.

v0.5 · THIS BUILD

Definition‑class interpolation + cross‑class halo; drift z with error bars + elevation damping; ±3–5 d noise floor

Sources tagged by what they measure; within‑class median for the centre, cross‑class spread for the halo. Mountain drift damped to a [0.3–0.7] range; the noise‑floor rule written into the method.

Stops a single photography blog from masquerading as precision, and stops alpine sites inheriting a lowland trend. Principle: a halo that widens with thin data is information, not noise to hide.

v0.5 · THIS BUILD

Anchor year y_op = 2005.5; provenance ambiguity carried in the halo; Alishan toggle retired

Central line on the WMO 1991–2020 centre; 1981–2010 retired to a footnote (would double‑count the trend). The Alishan "past‑peak vs on‑peak" fork now lives inside its shown halo with a Diagnosis note, so the binary toggle is removed.

A continuous shown band is strictly better than a two‑state switch, and per‑site vintages we can't verify would be fabricated — so the ambiguity is displayed, not faked. Principle: show uncertainty; never assert a precision you don't have.
